Frontlines

Two kingdoms, one river. You never command a soldier. You draw the paths they march on, and the armies sort out the rest.

Draw the march

Drag from a blue town to draw a march route. Tap a town to choose its troops or clear its route.

Attack or gather

End a route on an enemy or grey town to attack it, or on open ground to gather an army. Drag from a gathered army to send it on.

Strength in numbers

Big groups hit harder, troops arriving at a run hit hardest, and everyone fights better near home.

Gold and building

Your towns and gold mines earn gold. Farms grow food, and towns need food to train soldiers quickly. Spend gold in the tray on farms, watchtowers, bridges and barracks. A mine works for whoever holds the land around it.

Terrain matters

Roads are 40% faster. Archers on hills shoot further, and attacking uphill is weaker. Forests slow troops but shelter them from arrows. Fords let anyone wade across, slowly.

Learn as you play

Hold Shift and point at anything to see what it does and how it works: towns and ground, gold, every button in the tray. Press Space to pause first.

Know your enemy

The enemy gathers at the bridges and sometimes fakes an attack. Take Redmarsh to win; lose Ashford and the war is over.

    How Frontlines works

    Rules at a glance

    Orders

    • Drag from a town to draw its march route. New soldiers follow it. The line snaps to roads; hold Shift to draw freehand.
    • End on a town to attack it, or on open ground to gather an army there.
    • Drag from a gathered army to send it on.
    • Tap a town to pick its troops or clear its route.
    • Charge (first in the tray, or press C; every 25s): your troops in the circle run at the enemy where you click, sprinting, and their first blow hits two to three times as hard.
    • Space pauses, Esc cancels. Hold Shift and hover to inspect anything.
    • Scroll or pinch to zoom in on the fighting, drag open ground to look around, double-click to zoom in or back out.

    Troops

    • Spearmen ▲ tough, stop cavalry charges. Beat cavalry.
    • Archers ● shoot from range, fragile up close. Beat spearmen.
    • Cavalry ■ fast, triple charge damage, train slowly. Beat archers.
    • Companies: 6 or more gathered together form up under a banner, facing the enemy: spearmen in front, archers behind, cavalry on the wings. When any of them fights, they all join in.
    • Volleys: a company with 8 or more archers shoots together every 4 s, a rain of arrows on the thickest crowd of enemies, far beyond one archer's reach. Close-packed blocks suffer most; charging cavalry mostly outrun it.
    • Shield wall: a company with 8 or more spearmen holds its ground. They fight two ranks deep, take 35% less in hand-to-hand fighting, and cavalry charging them get hurt. Arrows still break it.
    • Riding out: a company with 6 or more riders rides out at enemy archers and cavalry attacking your troops nearby, wheels off to charge again, and comes back to its place.

    Combat

    • Each nearby ally adds 6% damage, up to +60%.
    • Troops fight 40% tougher near their own towns.
    • A town falls when enough enemies march into it to empty its defence ring.

    Terrain

    • Roads: march 40% faster.
    • Woods: 35% slower, arrows do half damage.
    • Hills: archers +10 range per contour; downhill hits +25%, uphill −20%.
    • Fords: anyone can wade across at 45% speed.

    Economy

    • Towns earn about 14 gold a week each (a capital 20) for whoever holds them.
    • Farms grow food: about every 36 s a cart brings 5 to the farm's town (it stores up to 40). Each soldier trained eats 1; a town out of food trains at a quarter of its speed.
    • Gold mines earn 27 a week for whoever holds the land around them.
    • Buy in the tray: farm 30, watchtower 40, barracks 70, bridge 80. Each repeat costs 50% more.
    • Each week: one free reinforcement, and the enemy grows stronger.
